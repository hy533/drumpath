# 4/4 Time Signature

## What you need to know

The 4/4 time signature is the most common in Western music — so common it is nicknamed "common time." The top number (4) tells you there are 4 beats per bar. The bottom number (4) tells you the beat unit is a quarter note (one tap of your foot).

Count aloud: **1 — 2 — 3 — 4 — 1 — 2 — 3 — 4**. That cycle is one bar, repeating forever until the song ends.

**Standard rock beat placement in 4/4:**

- Bass drum: beats 1 and 3 (the "downbeats")
- Snare drum: beats 2 and 4 (the "backbeat")
- Hi-hat: every beat, or every eighth note (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 +)

A bar of 4/4 contains exactly 4 quarter-note beats of rhythmic content. You can fill it with any combination of note values that add up to 4 beats: four quarter notes, eight eighth notes, one whole note, two half notes, or any mixture.

## Key points

- Top number = beats per bar (4)
- Bottom number = which note value equals one beat (4 = quarter note)
- Count: 1, 2, 3, 4 — repeat
- Backbeat = snare on 2 and 4 (the "feel" of most rock and pop)
- Downbeat = beat 1 (the strongest beat, often bass drum)
- Eighth notes split each beat in two: "1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and"
