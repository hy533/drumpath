# Single Stroke Roll

## What you need to know

The single stroke roll (RLRL…) is the foundation of all drumming. Every note alternates hands: right, left, right, left. Despite its simplicity, mastering the single stroke roll means mastering evenness — each stroke identical in volume, tone, and timing.

Start slowly on a practice pad. Focus on making every stroke sound the same. The most common beginner mistake is letting the dominant hand play louder or faster than the other. Record yourself and listen back — any accent you hear is unevenness you cannot feel in the moment.

**How to build speed:**

1. Play RLRL at 60 BPM on a pad — aim for perfect evenness before adding speed
2. Increase by 4–5 BPM only when you sound even at the current tempo
3. Use a metronome every session; your internal clock is less reliable than you think
4. Above ~120 BPM, shift from wrist-driven to rebound-driven strokes — let the stick bounce

Full-speed single strokes (180–220 BPM) rely on the Moeller technique and finger control far more than raw arm strength. The stick does much of the work if you stay relaxed.

## Key points

- Sticking: R L R L (or L R L R — lead with your weaker hand for balance)
- Every stroke must be equal in volume and timing
- Slow + even beats fast + sloppy every time
- Metronome is mandatory — not optional
- Rebound (letting the stick bounce back) is what enables high speed
- Practice with accents on beat 1, then 2, then 3, then 4 to expose weaknesses
