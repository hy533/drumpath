# Matched Grip

## What you need to know

Matched grip is the most widely used drumstick grip in contemporary drumming. Both hands hold the stick the same way — hence "matched." It evolved from marching band technique and has become the default grip in rock, pop, jazz, and orchestral percussion.

The stick rests between the pad of your thumb and the side of your index finger, about one-third of the way from the butt end. The remaining fingers curl loosely underneath for control. When you play, the stick rebounds naturally off the drum head — your job is to guide it, not grip it tight.

There are three matched-grip sub-styles:

- **German grip** — palms face down, gives maximum power
- **American grip** — palms at 45°, balances power and control (most common for beginners)
- **French grip** — palms face each other (thumbs on top), best for finger control and delicate playing

Start with American grip. It is versatile enough for most styles and transitions naturally to the others.

## Key points

- Both hands hold the stick identically
- Grip point: roughly one-third from the butt end
- Hold lightly — the stick should bounce freely off the head
- Fingers curl underneath for secondary support and rebound control
- Wrist drives power strokes; fingers guide finesse strokes
- Avoid "death grip" — tension kills speed and causes injury over time
